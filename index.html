Why now

The shift from search to answers

A woman who suspects she is low in iron no longer types a few words into Google and picks a link. More and more, she describes how she feels to an assistant such as ChatGPT and reads one answer. The question for the clinic is no longer only how to rank on a results page. It is whether that answer names The Iron Clinic.

Before
iron deficiency symptoms women

Ten links. She clicks two or three. Being on the first page is the whole game.

→
Now
I'm exhausted all the time and my periods are really heavy. Could it be my iron, and where in London can I get it treated?

One written answer. It either names a clinic and explains why, or it does not.

Say it the way she asks it

Every post answers a real question in her own words, so the clinic's content matches how the question is put to an assistant.

One story, told the same way

The same details, in the same words, on the website, on every platform and in every post. An assistant that finds two versions trusts neither.

Named expertise, with sources

Content carries Professor Richards's name and links to guidelines and published research, which is what an answer can cite and a reader can check.

Nobody can promise that an assistant will name a clinic. What can be measured is whether it does, month by month, and that check is part of the monthly report below.

Client question · two slides
Slide 1
Can my iron be low if my blood count is normal?
Slide 2
Yes. Haemoglobin, the number on a standard blood count, is usually the last thing to fall. Your iron stores, measured by ferritin, can be low for a long time before it does. That stage is called iron deficiency without anaemia, and it can still leave you tired.
Caption
One of the questions we hear most. A normal blood count does not always mean normal iron. If you have symptoms, ask whether your ferritin has been checked. General information, not medical advice.

Safeguards

Clinical and advertising safeguards

No medicine brands in public posts

Prescription-only medicines cannot be advertised to the public in the United Kingdom (Human Medicines Regulations 2012). Posts talk about intravenous iron in general; any comparison of named products stays in material for clinicians.

Referenced, and general

I keep each claim with its source, and every post carries "general information, not medical advice". Posts point to a doctor, never replace one.

One agreed set of clinic details

I take clinic numbers such as doctors, locations and infusions given from one agreed list, so posts never contradict the website.

Accountability

The monthly report

One page from me in the first week of each month, saying what worked, what did not, and what I will change next month.

Reach

How many people saw the posts, and how many new followers each platform gained.

Interest

Saves, shares and comments, which show what people found worth keeping or passing on.

Visits

Clicks through to theironclinic.com from each platform, and which posts sent them.

Enquiries

Booking enquiries that mention social media, if the clinic can share that number.

Answer check

Ten questions women ask about iron, put to ChatGPT and similar assistants each month, with a record of whether The Iron Clinic is named in the answer.

Next month

The three changes the numbers point to, which I agree with you before I plan the next month.
